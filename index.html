Magnolia prices $500 million notes offering tied to pending WildFire Energy acquisition

Event date: July 22, 2026. Source publication date: July 22, 2026. Underlying agreement: announced July 20, 2026.

  • Magnolia Oil & Gas Operating LLC and Magnolia Oil & Gas Finance Corp. priced a $500 million private offering of 6.625% senior unsecured notes due 2034.
  • The financing update relates to Magnolia Oil & Gas Corp.’s pending acquisition of WildFire Energy. Magnolia’s July 20 announcement put the transaction value at approximately $4.06 billion, inclusive of WildFire debt, subject to customary adjustments.
  • Magnolia agreed to acquire 100% of WildFire Intermediate Holdings from WildFire Energy I LLC for $2.65 billion cash, 32.203 million Magnolia Class A shares, and assumption of $600 million of WildFire 7.50% notes due 2029. Closing remains subject to customary conditions, including HSR clearance; Magnolia expects late-Q3 2026 closing.
  • Magnolia said WildFire brings about 810,000 net Giddings-area acres, roughly 53,000 BOE/d (about 70% oil), a sand mine and more than 500 miles of gas-gathering pipelines.

Why it matters: This is a significant South Texas consolidation across producing wells, gathering and supply infrastructure. If it closes, well/master-data, ownership and allocation, production-accounting, measurement, vendor and field-workflow records will need disciplined integration; the financing itself does not mean the acquisition has closed.

Public sources: Magnolia July 22 notes-pricing 8-K · Magnolia July 20 acquisition release filed with the SEC

EIA: U.S. commercial crude inventories rise 2.0 million barrels, remain below five-year average

Reference week ended: July 17, 2026. Source publication date: July 22, 2026.

  • U.S. commercial crude oil inventories excluding the Strategic Petroleum Reserve increased 2.0 million barrels week over week to 411.7 million barrels.
  • EIA said crude inventories were about 6% below the five-year average for that time of year.
  • Refineries operated at 96.1% of operable capacity; total commercial petroleum inventories increased 11.6 million barrels.

Why it matters: The inventory build adds a near-term balance signal, but sub-five-year crude stocks and high refinery utilization make weekly interpretation sensitive to runs, imports and product movements. Operators should reconcile commercial inventory context with their own nominations, sales volumes, timing and measurement data rather than treating it as a field-output proxy.

Public source: EIA Weekly Petroleum Status Report — July 22 release
